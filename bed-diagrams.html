Planting Diagrams — 20' Layout

Six 4×4 bed sections, the asparagus/perennial bed, the garlic plot, and the in-ground center. Grid lines are 1 ft.

Bed 1a — Tomato

4 plants · 2 ft spacing (2×2 grid)

Bed 1b — Pepper + Basil

6 pepper (~16 in apart) · 4 basil tucked between

Bed 2a — Cucumber (trellised)

4 plants along back trellis, ~12 in apart

Bed 2b — Zucchini / Summer Squash

2 plants, ~8 sq ft each — tight fit, expect sprawl

Bed 3a — Carrot / Beet / Radish

3 rows: carrot 2×15 (~3 in), beet 1×16 (~3-4 in), radish 2×16 (~2-3 in). Gold dashed edge = onion sets bordering the bed.

Bed 3b — Peas (spring planting)

16 plants along back trellis, ~3 in apart

Bed 3b — Bush Beans (summer succession)

12 plants, 4 across × 3 rows, same footprint after peas are pulled

Asparagus / Perennial Bed

10 asparagus crowns in 2 rows of 5 (~12 in apart within row, rows 18 in apart — tighter than the ideal 3 ft, but workable in a small bed) · 3 strawberries along the front edge, ~12 in apart. Garlic moved out to its own dedicated plot.

In-Ground Center (13×10 ft)

Corn block (32, 4×8 grid) with pole beans interplanted at every other stalk (16) · Potato block (16, 2×8) · 1 pumpkin hill · 4 melon hills (compact/trellis type) filling the space freed up from the other 2 squash hills

Garlic Plot (5×2)

14 cloves, 2 rows of 7 (~8-9 in apart within row), rows ~12 in apart. Fall-planted (mid-late Oct), harvested the following July.

Beds 2a (cucumber) and 3b (peas → beans) are trellised, so their ground footprint is only lightly used — good spots for a quick underplanting like radish or lettuce. Bed 2b (squash) is the tightest of the six; if it feels overcrowded by midsummer, dropping to 1 plant is a reasonable adjustment. In the in-ground center, the pumpkin hill and 4 melon hills are shown with their approximate vine-spread radius (dashed) — those vines will sprawl over the corn/potato rows and open ground as the season goes on, which is normal. Melons here assume a compact/short-season variety (e.g. 'Minnesota Midget') given how little spare ground this zone actually has.
